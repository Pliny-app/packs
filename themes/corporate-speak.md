# Corporate Speak

## Meetings and internal talk
- **align · alignment** — agree on direction. *Often:* get the boss to approve.
- **sync** — a short meeting to share status.
- **touch base · check in** — talk briefly.
- **circle back** — return to a topic later. *Often:* never.
- **take it offline** — discuss outside this meeting. *Often:* stop talking about it here.
- **loop in · keep in the loop** — include someone; keep them informed.
- **level-set** — make sure everyone starts from the same facts.
- **double-click on** — look at something in more detail.
- **deep dive** — a thorough look at one topic.
- **unpack** — explain piece by piece.
- **zoom out · step back · the big picture** — look at the whole situation instead of the details; also the **30,000-foot view** or **helicopter view**.
- **in the weeds · granular** — deep in the details; the opposite of zooming out.
- **bandwidth · capacity · cycles** — time and attention available. "I don't have the bandwidth" means no.
- **hard stop** — a fixed time someone must leave.
- **parking lot** — a list of topics set aside for later.
- **put a pin in it** — set something aside for now.
- **action item · next steps** — tasks agreed in a meeting.
- **owner** — the person responsible. Apple's **DRI** (directly responsible individual) is the same idea.
- **take the lead on · take point on · own** — be responsible for something. *Take point* comes from military patrols, where the point man walks first.
- **step in · step up** — take over when someone is absent; take on more responsibility. *Step up* often comes before a promotion, or instead of one.
- **the ask** — the request, as a noun.
- **buy-in** — agreement and support from the people who matter.
- **socialize** — share an idea informally to win support before deciding.
- **run it up the flagpole** — test an idea on others.
- **ideate · blue-sky thinking** — brainstorm; think without limits.
- **learnings** — lessons. *Often:* what went wrong.
- **low-hanging fruit** — easy wins.
- **move the needle** — make a noticeable difference.
- **boil the ocean** — attempt something impossibly broad.
- **net-net · at the end of the day** — in summary; ultimately.
- **heads-up** — advance warning.
- **quick question** — a question. *Often:* not quick.

## Strategy and growth
- **synergy** — the whole worth more than its parts. *Often:* the reason for a merger, then for job cuts.
- **leverage** — use to advantage; in finance, borrowed money.
- **scale** — grow without costs growing as fast.
- **ecosystem** — products, partners and customers locked together.
- **value proposition** — why a customer should buy.
- **paradigm shift** — a fundamental change in how things are done.
- **disrupt · disruption** — overturn an industry with something cheaper or simpler.
- **pivot** — change strategy sharply. *Often:* the first plan failed.
- **iterate · ship · MVP** — improve in small steps; release; a *minimum viable product*, the simplest version worth testing.
- **double down** — commit more to a bet.
- **north star** — the guiding goal or metric.
- **roadmap** — the planned order of work.
- **pipeline** — deals, hires or products on the way.
- **runway · burn rate** — how long the money lasts; how fast it's spent.
- **headwinds · tailwinds** — forces against; forces in favor.
- **unlock value** — make something more profitable. *Often:* sell it or cut costs.
- **flywheel** — a self-reinforcing cycle of growth (from Jim Collins).
- **moat** — a lasting advantage competitors can't easily cross (from Warren Buffett).
- **moonshot · 10x** — a wildly ambitious goal; a tenfold improvement.
- **verticals** — markets or industries a company sells into.
- **go-to-market (GTM)** — the plan for selling a product.
- **core competency** — what a company does best.
- **mission-critical** — essential; the business stops without it.

## Buzz adjectives
- **innovative · cutting-edge · next-generation** — new. *Often:* not very.
- **best-in-class · world-class · industry-leading** — the best. *Often:* unverified.
- **robust · seamless · end-to-end · holistic** — solid; smooth; complete; all-round.
- **scalable · agile · nimble** — able to grow; quick to adapt.
- **customer-centric · purpose-driven · values-led** — focused on customers; driven by a mission; guided by principles.
- **impactful · actionable** — having an effect; usable.
- **transformational** — changing everything.

## People and HR
- **talent · human capital · resources · headcount** — people, as assets, costs or a number.
- **team member · associate · partner** — employee.
- **onboarding · offboarding** — bringing someone in; seeing them out.
- **hit the ground running** — be productive from the first day. *Often:* don't expect much onboarding.
- **growth area · opportunity** — a weakness.
- **feedback · 360 review · calibration** — evaluation; review by peers, reports and managers; managers ranking people against each other.
- **high performer · A-player** — strong employee.
- **culture fit** — matches the team's values. *Often:* similar to the people already there.
- **flight risk** — likely to quit.
- **regrettable attrition** — losing people the company wanted to keep.
- **backfill** — hire to replace someone who left.
- **performance improvement plan (PIP)** — a formal warning with goals. *Often:* the first step out.
- **managed out** — pushed to leave without being fired.
- **garden leave** — paid notice spent away from work (chiefly UK).
- **we're a family** — we're close. *Often:* expect loyalty beyond the contract.
- **skip-level** — a meeting with your manager's manager.
- **individual contributor (IC)** — someone with no direct reports.

## Cuts and bad news
- **restructuring · reorganization · reorg** — changing the structure. *Often:* layoffs.
- **rightsizing · workforce optimization · reduction in force (RIF)** — layoffs.
- **efficiencies · cost discipline · streamline** — spending less.
- **letting go · parting ways · transition** — firing or departure.
- **pursuing other opportunities · spending more time with family** — leaving. *Often:* not by choice.
- **sunset · deprecate · end of life** — stop a product: announce it's ending; phase it out; final.
- **pause · deprioritize · revisit** — stop working on something. *Often:* for good.
- **exit a market** — withdraw from it.
- **softer demand · challenging environment · macro headwinds** — sales fell.
- **negative growth** — shrinkage.
- **below expectations** — a miss.
- **one-time charge · non-recurring · write-down · impairment** — a loss the company hopes you'll set aside; an asset revalued lower.
- **restatement** — earlier accounts corrected.
- **exploring strategic alternatives** — reviewing options. *Often:* looking for a buyer.

## Press releases
- **is pleased to announce · is excited to share · is proud to** — the opening formula.
- **dateline** — the city and date that open a release.
- **leading provider of · innovative solutions** — the self-description formula.
- **strategic partnership** — two companies working together; the details often vague.
- **empower · transform · reimagine · unlock** — the verbs of product launches.
- **journey · next chapter** — a change of direction or leadership.
- **said [Name], CEO of [Company]** — the quote formula, nearly always written by communications staff.
- **boilerplate** — the standard "About [Company]" paragraph at the end.
- **forward-looking statements · safe harbor** — the legal disclaimer that predictions may not come true.
- **embargo** — a time before which journalists may not publish.
- **###** — the traditional end mark of a release.
- **media contact** — the name and email at the very bottom.

## Crisis statements
- **we take [X] very seriously** — the standard opening. *Often:* we've been criticized for not doing so.
- **out of an abundance of caution** — as a precaution. *Often:* we're not sure how bad it is.
- **isolated incident** — a one-off.
- **does not reflect our values** — we disown it.
- **we apologize for any inconvenience** — the minimal apology.
- **we're sorry if anyone was offended** — a **non-apology**: regret for the reaction, not the act.
- **mistakes were made** — the passive voice hides who made them. The political analyst William Schneider called it the "past exonerative" tense.
- **a security incident · unauthorized access** — a breach.
- **service disruption · degraded performance** — an outage.
- **no evidence to suggest** — we haven't found any. *Often:* not the same as "it didn't happen."
- **we are reviewing our processes · conducting a thorough investigation** — we're looking into it.
- **cooperating fully with authorities** — under investigation.
- **independent review · third-party audit** — an outside check, often commissioned by the company.
- **we hold ourselves accountable** — the promise; consequences seldom specified.
- **our thoughts are with** — the condolence formula.
- **declined to comment · no comment** — refusing to answer, as reported by the press.

## Email and memo
- **hope this finds you well** — the opener.
- **just following up · gentle reminder · friendly reminder · bumping this** — a nudge, rising in impatience.
- **per my last email · as previously mentioned** — I already told you.
- **as discussed** — confirming in writing.
- **please find attached** — the attachment formula.
- **looping in · moving to bcc** — adding someone; quietly removing the crowd.
- **closing the loop** — confirming something's done.
- **thanks in advance** — please do this.
- **TL;DR · BLUF · executive summary** — the short version; *bottom line up front*, from US military writing; a summary for leaders.
- **FYI · EOD · EOW · OOO** — for your information; end of day; end of week; out of office.
- **Best · Kind regards · Cheers** — sign-offs, cooler to warmer (*Cheers* is British-casual).

## Titles and structure
- **C-suite** — the top executives: CEO, CFO, COO, CTO, CMO. **CPO** can mean product or people.
- **VP · SVP · EVP** — vice president; senior; executive.
- **head of · lead · principal** — titles for running an area, a team, or senior expertise.
- **evangelist · advocate** — someone paid to promote a product or platform.
- **matrix organization · dotted line** — people reporting to more than one boss; the second, informal reporting line.
- **steering committee · task force · tiger team · war room** — senior oversight; a group for one problem; a small expert squad; crisis headquarters.
- **center of excellence** — a team that sets standards for the rest.
- **RACI** — who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted and Informed.

## Acronyms and metrics
- **KPI · OKR** — key performance indicator; objectives and key results (from Intel, popularized by Google).
- **ROI · P&L** — return on investment; profit and loss.
- **EBITDA** — earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization.
- **ARR · MRR · run rate** — annual and monthly recurring revenue; a short period scaled up to a year.
- **YoY · QoQ · H1 · H2 · FY** — year over year; quarter over quarter; first and second half; fiscal year.
- **CAGR · TAM** — compound annual growth rate; total addressable market.
- **NPS · CSAT · churn** — net promoter score; customer satisfaction; customers lost.
- **DAU · MAU** — daily and monthly active users.
- **SLA · NDA · MoU · LOI · SOW · RFP** — service-level agreement; non-disclosure agreement; memorandum of understanding; letter of intent; statement of work; request for proposal.
- **B2B · B2C · SaaS** — selling to businesses; to consumers; software as a service.
- **WFH · RTO** — working from home; return to office.

## Words about the register
- **corporate speak · management-speak · bureaucratese** — the language as a whole.
- **jargon · buzzword** — insider vocabulary; a fashionable term used more for effect than meaning.
- **euphemism** — a soft word for a hard thing.
- **weasel words** — words that sound like a commitment but aren't: *up to*, *helps*, *may*.
- **hedging** — qualifying every claim to avoid being pinned down.
- **spin · PR-speak** — framing bad news favorably.
- **doublespeak** — language meant to hide meaning; a 1950s blend of Orwell's *doublethink* and *newspeak*.
- **gobbledygook** — pompous, unclear official language; coined by US congressman Maury Maverick in 1944.
- **word salad** — words strung together without real meaning.
- **legalese** — dense legal wording.
- **buzzword bingo** — the game of ticking off clichés during a meeting.

## Strongly tied to one company
These phrases are free to use, but readers may hear the source.
- **bias for action · disagree and commit · two-pizza team** — Amazon (*disagree and commit* goes back to Intel).
- **squads · tribes · guilds** — the "Spotify model" of team structure.
- **year of efficiency** — Meta, 2023.
- **DRI** — Apple.

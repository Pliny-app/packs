# Alien Worlds

## Kinds of world
- **terrestrial / rocky planet** — a world of rock and metal, like Earth or Mars.
- **super-Earth** — a rocky world larger than Earth but smaller than Neptune.
- **sub-Neptune / mini-Neptune** — a small world with a thick gas envelope; among the most common planets found around other stars, yet absent from our own system.
- **gas giant** — a huge world of hydrogen and helium with no solid surface, like Jupiter.
- **ice giant** — a giant made mostly of water, ammonia and methane ices, like Uranus and Neptune.
- **hot Jupiter** — a gas giant orbiting so close to its star that its year lasts days.
- **puffy planet** — a gas giant so swollen it's less dense than cork.
- **ocean world / water world** — a planet or moon covered, or filled, by water.
- **hycean world** — a proposed type: a warm ocean planet under a hydrogen-rich sky.
- **lava world** — a world so hot its surface is molten rock.
- **desert world** — dry from pole to pole.
- **ice world / snowball planet** — frozen over; Earth itself may once have been a *Snowball Earth*.
- **carbon planet** — theorized: rich in carbon, perhaps with layers of graphite and diamond.
- **iron planet** — theorized: mostly metal core, little rock.
- **chthonian planet** — theorized: the bare core of a gas giant stripped by its star.
- **rogue planet** — a world with no star, drifting alone through the dark.
- **circumbinary planet** — a world orbiting two stars.
- **tidally locked** — always showing one face to its star: endless day on one side, endless night on the other.
- **eyeball planet** — a theorized tidally locked world, iced over except for an open sea under its sun.
- **moon** — a world orbiting a planet. **Exomoon**: a moon around a planet of another star.
- **dwarf planet** — round, but too small to clear its orbit, like Pluto.
- **exoplanet** — any planet orbiting a star other than the Sun.

## Star and orbit
- **host star / primary** — the star a world circles.
- **red dwarf** — a small, cool, long-lived star; the most common kind, and prone to violent flares.
- **yellow dwarf** — a Sun-like star.
- **binary · trinary** — two stars bound together; three.
- **habitable zone / Goldilocks zone** — the band around a star where liquid water could exist on a surface.
- **orbit · orbital period** — the path around a star; the length of a year.
- **eccentric orbit** — a stretched, oval orbit that brings fierce seasons.
- **axial tilt** — the lean of a planet's axis; what makes seasons.
- **sol** — a day on Mars; borrowed for any planet's day.
- **terminator** — the line between day and night on a world's face.
- **conjunction · eclipse · transit** — two bodies lining up in the sky; one blocking another; a planet crossing its star's face.
- **Lagrange points** — five places where gravity balances, good for stations.
- **ring system** — bands of ice and rock around a planet. All four giants in our system have rings.
- **shepherd moon** — a small moon that keeps a ring's edge sharp.
- **Roche limit** — the distance inside which a moon is torn apart by its planet's pull.
- **naming** — exoplanets are named for their star plus a lowercase letter, starting with *b*: Proxima Centauri b.

## Sky and atmosphere
- **atmosphere** — the layer of gas around a world. It can be *thin, thick, crushing, breathable, toxic, corrosive* or *absent*.
- **airless** — with no atmosphere; the sky is black even in daylight.
- **breathable** — air humans could breathe unaided; rare, and a huge plot point.
- **runaway greenhouse** — heat trapped until oceans boil away, as on Venus.
- **haze** — a smog of particles; Titan's is orange.
- **cloud deck** — a thick, solid-looking layer of cloud.
- **sulfuric · ammonia · methane** — the chemistries of alien clouds.
- **superrotation** — an atmosphere spinning faster than the planet beneath it, as on Venus.
- **magnetosphere** — a magnetic shield against the stellar wind; Mars lost most of its air without one.
- **aurora** — light curtains where charged particles meet the air.
- **radiation belt** — a zone of trapped, deadly particles around a planet.
- **albedo** — how much light a surface reflects; ice is bright, basalt dark.
- **sky colors** — reddish-tan by day on Mars, with blue sunsets; yellow under Venus's clouds; orange on Titan.

## Weather
- **dust storm** — on Mars, sometimes large enough to wrap the whole planet.
- **megastorm** — a storm larger than Earth; Jupiter's Great Red Spot has raged for centuries.
- **jet stream · band** — fast rivers of wind; the colored stripes of gas giants.
- **methane rain** — falls on Titan, filling lakes and rivers.
- **acid rain** — on Venus, sulfuric acid, which evaporates before it lands.
- **glass rain** — believed to blow sideways on the hot Jupiter HD 189733b.
- **iron rain** — thought to fall on the night side of the hot Jupiter WASP-76b.
- **diamond rain** — proposed deep inside ice giants.
- **stellar flare** — a sudden blast from the star that scours a planet's surface.
- **tidal heating** — a moon kneaded by its planet's gravity until it melts inside; it makes Io the most volcanic body known.

## Terrain
- **regolith** — the loose dust and broken rock blanketing an airless surface.
- **crater · impact basin · ejecta** — the scar of a strike; a huge one; the debris it threw out.
- **maria** — the Moon's dark plains, once mistaken for seas (singular: *mare*).
- **highlands** — old, cratered upland.
- **shield volcano** — a broad, gently sloped volcano; Olympus Mons on Mars is the tallest in the solar system.
- **caldera** — the collapsed bowl atop a volcano.
- **lava tube** — a hollow left by flowing lava; a natural shelter.
- **rift valley · chasm · canyon** — the land pulled apart. Mars's Valles Marineris would span the United States.
- **mesa · butte** — a flat-topped hill; a smaller, steeper one.
- **erg · dune sea** — a vast field of sand dunes.
- **salt flat** — the white crust left by a dried sea.
- **tessera** — rumpled, criss-crossed terrain, named on Venus.
- **chaos terrain** — jumbled blocks of ice on Europa, broken and refrozen.
- **cryovolcano** — a volcano erupting water, ammonia or methane instead of lava.
- **geyser / plume** — jets of vapor and ice, like those on Saturn's moon Enceladus.
- **subsurface ocean** — liquid water under an icy shell; Europa and Enceladus probably have one.
- **ice shelf · glacier · permafrost** — floating ice; flowing ice; ground frozen year-round.
- **badlands** — eroded, gullied, barren ground.
- **karst** — rock dissolved into caves, sinkholes and spires.

## Life
- **biosphere** — all the life on a world, taken together.
- **biosignature** — a sign of life seen from afar, such as oxygen and methane together in an atmosphere.
- **extremophile** — life thriving where it shouldn't: boiling, freezing, acid or crushing depths.
- **chemosynthesis** — life fed by chemical energy instead of sunlight, as around deep-sea vents.
- **bioluminescence** — light made by living things.
- **xenoflora · xenofauna** — alien plants; alien animals.
- **megafauna** — large animals.
- **microbial mat** — layered sheets of microbes; perhaps the most likely alien life.
- **silicon-based life** — speculative life built on silicon instead of carbon.
- **panspermia** — the idea that life spreads between worlds on rocks and comets.
- **ecosystem · food web · apex predator** — how life connects; who eats whom; who eats last.
- **monoculture** — a world, or a field, of a single species.

## Arrival and settlement
- **survey · probe · lander · rover** — the steps of first contact with a world.
- **landfall** — the first landing.
- **outpost · colony · settlement** — from small and provisional to permanent.
- **hab / habitat module** — a sealed living unit.
- **dome · biodome** — a sealed shelter for air, warmth and crops.
- **pressure suit · rebreather** — what lets people walk outside.
- **airlock** — the double door between inside and out.
- **terraforming** — remaking a world to suit Earth life; the word was coined by sci-fi writer Jack Williamson in 1942.
- **paraterraforming** — roofing over part of a world instead of changing all of it.
- **arcology** — a city in one huge, self-contained structure.
- **spaceport · orbital station · space elevator** — the ways on and off a world.
- **generation ship** — a ship whose voyage outlasts the lives of its first crew.
- **in-situ resources** — water, metals and fuel gathered on site instead of shipped in.
- **xenoarchaeology** — the study of ruins left by alien civilizations.

## Describing a world
- **barren · desolate · lifeless** — empty; bleak; without life.
- **hostile · inhospitable** — dangerous; unwilling to support life.
- **habitable · Earthlike · temperate** — livable; similar to home; mild.
- **verdant · lush · teeming** — green; richly grown; crowded with life.
- **frozen · glacial · frost-bound** — locked in cold.
- **scorched · sun-blasted · molten** — burned; harshly lit; liquid with heat.
- **arid · parched** — dry; dried out.
- **storm-wracked · wind-scoured** — battered by weather; worn by wind.
- **sulfurous · toxic · caustic** — reeking of sulfur; poisonous; corrosive.
- **airless · pressureless** — without atmosphere.
- **ringed · cratered · banded** — with rings; pocked with impacts; striped, like a gas giant.
- **twilit** — in permanent dusk, as on a tidally locked world's terminator.
- **alien · uncanny** — wholly foreign; almost familiar, but wrong.

## Real worlds as reference
- **Venus** — crushing heat and pressure under acid clouds.
- **Mars** — cold, thin-aired red desert with giant volcanoes and canyons.
- **Titan** — Saturn's moon, with a thick orange haze and methane lakes.
- **Europa · Enceladus** — icy moons hiding oceans.
- **Io** — Jupiter's moon, the most volcanic body known.
- **Proxima Centauri b** — the nearest known exoplanet, orbiting a red dwarf.
- **TRAPPIST-1** — a red dwarf with seven rocky planets, several in its habitable zone.

## Borrowed from specific works
- **Class M planet** (Star Trek) - *Earthlike, habitable*
- **Tatooine**, **Hoth** (Star Wars) - Astronomers do nickname planets with two suns "Tatooines."

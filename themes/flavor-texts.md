# Flavor Texts
A cheat sheet for in-game flavour texts and item descriptions 

# Setup
## Narrative Goals
*How does the item fit the narrative and helps it goals?*
- Give clues to solve puzzles: "Among other uses for this plants, the most remarkable is that it can allow a person to pass unnoticed" - Strange Horticulture
- Contribute to worldbuilding: "The exact process of how an Altered Item is born eludes us" - Control
- Move the story forward

## Catalog
### Type
*How does the game explain that items have descriptions?*
#### Diegetic:
*Catalogs with in-world explanation*
- A book given players by an in-world author (The Strange book of plants in Strange Horticolture, Hunter's Journal in Hollow Knight)
- Players collect files, letters, reports from an archive, scattered around the world (Control)
- A field journal filled during game play. Players or their avatars explore, make observations, or complete actions that add entries to the journal (scanning samples in In Other Waters, the ship Log in Outer Wilds)
- The protagonist's intuition or perception of the item (skills comment on what the player does in Disco Elysium)
- Sentient objects who talk to the player (no examples found)

### Non-Diegetic
*Texts are a game feature with no in-world explanation*
- Inventory texts ("Rusted iron key. Opens a locked door in Stormveil Castle", Elden Ring)

### Voice, Point of View, and Tense
*Who wrote the text, for whom, when, or why?*
* What is known about the author and it's character?
* Who is the audience of the text (the player, someone else)?
* When and where was the text written?

## Information Quality
*How should player understand the quality of the texts?*
### Completeness
- Complete: full knowledge or as an attempt to render the most complete understanding of the object (Strange Horticulture)
- Incomplete: some information is missing or redacted (Control)
- Progressive: more text is added during the game (the Hunter's notes appear after players kill enough of the same enemy) 

### Truth
*How reliable is the information?*
- Most information is true
- Information is given by a well meaning but unreliable author
- Contradicting sources that disagree on an item
- Hearsay of folklore vs science: what people believe vs what scientist record
- Outdated information, no longer true

### Structure
*How are texts structured and presented?*
* Description first, then use ("A frozen iron key. Opens the iron grating on the upper level of the Lamenter’s Gaol.", Elden Ring)
* Mechanics first, then flavor (“At the end of combat, heal 6 HP. Your body’s own blood burns with an undying rage", Slay The Spire)
* Description, then mechanics, then lore, then a closing sentence (Flask of Crimson Tears, Elden Ring)
* A story told across different objects (the armor Artorias set, Dark Souls)
 
# Language
## Genre-Specific Language
The register, tone and style match how people speak in the world

## Domain-Specific Language
Items use language from a the domain they belong to: botany for plants, geology for minerals, military for weapons,…

## Catalog-Specific
Item use the language of the type of catalog or media they belong to
- The bureaucratic or office language of an archive of case files, recordings, reports, correspondence (Control)
- Corporate speak ("Rock hard snacks, rock hard abs. +2 HP.", Going Under)
- A hunter's notes focusing on behaviors ("Crawlid: Timid scavenger that crawls through the caverns looking for food.", Hollow Knight)

## Object-Specific
The flavor text renders the actual text carried by the item such as labels, inscriptions, the words of a letter or paragraphs form a book.

## Notations and Extras
- Names for plants and animals in other languages (Latin, Greek, fantasy language)
- Numbers: catalog, case or sample numbers. Dates
- Locations of provenience or collection
* Closing quotes or Mottoes ("To seek the Elden Ring", Elden Ring)

## Perception
*How does the item register to the senses?*
- Sight (colors, shapes, materials)
- Touch (textures, "Gnarled and knotty", Elden Ring)
- Weight
- Temperature
- Smell
- Sound: noises when used, or calls and voiced of creatures
- Taste
- Light (emits light, glows, shines)
- What is missing: no odor, no sound, no weight
- Alterations: damage, worn from usage, modifications, custom parts

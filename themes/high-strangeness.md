# High Strangeness

*High strangeness* is J. Allen Hynek's term from UFO research for encounters so bizarre that no ordinary explanation fits. The stranger the details, the "higher" the case. Entries below note when a word is real (science, history, folklore) or a genre coinage. Terms owned by a single franchise are kept together at the end, so you can tell when you're borrowing.

## The strange itself
- **anomaly / anomalous** — something that breaks the expected pattern; the genre's neutral, clinical catch-all.
- **Fortean** — of the unexplained oddities science ignores, after Charles Fort, who collected them.
- **paranormal** — beyond what science can currently explain. Compare **supernatural**: beyond nature itself, with a magical or religious tinge.
- **uncanny** — familiar but wrong, like a face that's almost right.
- **weird** — something that doesn't belong here. Mark Fisher's distinction: the weird is a presence that shouldn't be there.
- **eerie** — Fisher again: something where there should be nothing, or nothing where there should be something.
- **eldritch** — unearthly and unsettling; strongly linked to Lovecraft.
- **numinous** — charged with the presence of something vast or holy; awe edged with fear.
- **liminal** — in between; on a threshold, neither one state nor the other.
- **manifestation** — the moment a hidden force takes visible or physical form.
- **entity** — a presence of unknown nature; deliberately vague.
- **the Veil** — the boundary between the ordinary world and what lies under it.
- **unexplained** — the plain word; often the most effective.

## Agencies and secrecy
- **black budget** — money spent on secret programs, hidden in public accounts.
- **black project** — a program funded that way and never acknowledged.
- **black site** — a facility that officially doesn't exist.
- **need-to-know** — information shared only with those who strictly require it.
- **compartmentalization** — splitting a secret so no single person sees all of it.
- **eyes only** — a marking: for the named reader and no one else.
- **classified / declassified** — locked under a secrecy level; later released, often heavily redacted.
- **redacted** — blacked out in a document; *redaction* is the black bar itself.
- **plausible deniability** — arranging things so those at the top can credibly say they didn't know.
- **dossier** — the file kept on a person or case.
- **field agent** — the one sent out to look; **handler** — the one who directs them.
- **asset** — a source or operative used by an agency, often expendable.
- **cleaner** — the person sent to remove evidence, or people.
- **legend** — spy term: a false identity with a fabricated past.
- **scrubbing** — erasing someone's records until they officially never existed; the result is an **unperson** (from Orwell).
- **debriefing** — formal questioning after an incident; in fiction, sometimes a polite word for something worse.
- **cabal / shadow government** — a hidden group steering events from behind official power.
- **Men in Black** — real folklore from the 1950s, before the films: dark-suited strangers who visit UFO witnesses and warn them to stay quiet.
- **Majestic 12 (MJ-12)** — an alleged secret UFO committee; the documents are widely judged forgeries.
- **Project Blue Book** — the real US Air Force study of UFO reports, 1952–1969.
- **MKUltra** — the real CIA program of mind-control experiments, 1950s–70s.
- **black helicopters** — unmarked aircraft of conspiracy lore, seen near incidents.
- **brutalism** — raw, massive concrete architecture; in this genre, the look of an institution that dwarfs the people inside it.

## Containment and cover-up
- **containment** — keeping an anomaly in, and the world out. A **containment breach** is when that fails.
- **protocol** — the fixed procedure for a situation; it sounds calm even when it isn't.
- **quarantine** — sealing off a place, or **information quarantine**: cutting phone lines and seizing drives so news can't leak.
- **cordon** — a perimeter guarded to keep people out.
- **cover story** — the official account offered instead of the truth: a gas leak, a training exercise.
- **sanitize / launder** — to rewrite a report until the anomaly disappears from it.
- **disinformation** — false information spread on purpose to mislead.
- **swamp gas / weather balloon** — classic official explanations for sightings, now shorthand for an unconvincing denial.
- **mass hysteria** — popular term for **mass psychogenic illness**: real symptoms spreading through a group with no physical cause. Useful as a cover story.
- **amnestic** — causing memory loss; in fiction, a drug or procedure that erases what a witness saw.
- **catch and release** — letting witnesses go but watching them, to see where the strangeness spreads.
- **Faraday cage** — a real metal enclosure that blocks electromagnetic signals.
- **ward / sigil** — a protective sign or symbol, the occult answer to a lock.

## Close encounters
- **UFO / UAP** — unidentified flying object; *unidentified anomalous phenomena* is the newer official term.
- **close encounter** — from Hynek's scale. *First kind*: a sighting at close range. *Second*: physical traces. *Third*: beings seen. A *fourth*, abduction, was added later by others.
- **abduction / abductee** — being taken by non-human beings, and the person who says it happened.
- **contactee** — someone who claims ongoing, often friendly, contact; a 1950s figure.
- **missing time** — a gap of hours the witness can't account for.
- **Oz factor** — Jenny Randles's term for the eerie stillness during an encounter: sound drops away and the world seems emptied.
- **greys** — the small, large-eyed beings of abduction lore.
- **implant** — a small object said to be placed in an abductee's body.
- **flap / wave** — a sudden cluster of sightings in one region.
- **window area** — a place with repeated sightings over the years.
- **foo fighters** — WWII pilots' name for strange lights that trailed their planes.
- **crop circle** — a geometric pattern flattened in a field; many were admitted hoaxes.
- **cattle mutilation** — livestock found with surgically precise wounds; a 1970s American mystery.
- **trace evidence** — burned grass, radiation or marks left at a landing site.

## Places and thresholds
- **liminal space** — a transitional place emptied of people, like a corridor, a car park at night or a closed mall, which feels wrong because of it.
- **threshold** — a doorway between one place, or one reality, and another.
- **portal** — a passage to somewhere else; more overt than a threshold.
- **dead zone** — an area where signals, electronics, and sometimes bodies stop working.
- **hotspot / vortex** — a site where strange events cluster; *vortex* adds New Age energy.
- **ley lines** — alignments of ancient sites, said by some to carry earth energy.
- **non-Euclidean** — breaking ordinary geometry, like a room bigger inside than out or angles that don't add up.
- **fold** — a bend in space joining two distant points; the impossible doorway, the corridor without end.
- **haunted / cursed** — the old words: a place or object that holds something.

## Time and reality
- **time slip** — briefly seeing or stepping into another era. A famous claim: two Englishwomen at Versailles in 1901.
- **chronal displacement** — a person or thing knocked forward or back in time.
- **loop** — a stretch of time that repeats.
- **glitch** — a moment when reality seems to stutter, as if badly rendered.
- **Mandela effect** — many people sharing the same false memory.
- **synchronicity** — Jung's term for meaningful coincidence with no causal link.
- **stone tape** — the idea that places record intense events and replay them, as echoes or after-images; named after a 1972 BBC play.
- **bleed** — strangeness seeping out of its confines into the ordinary world.
- **anchor** — an object or ritual that holds a person, or a place, in its own reality.
- **event horizon** — from black holes: the boundary past which nothing returns; used for the edge of an anomaly's reach.
- **parallel world / alternate timeline** — a version of reality next to ours.

## Bodies and life
- **body horror** — fear rooted in the body changing, breaking or being invaded.
- **chimera / chimerism** — in real biology, one body made of cells from two individuals; in fiction, a stitched-together creature.
- **hybrid** — part human, part something else.
- **biomechanical** — flesh and machine fused, in the style of H. R. Giger.
- **host** — a body something else lives in, or speaks through.
- **parasite / hive** — something that rides a host; a **hive** is many hosts serving one will.
- **xenobiology / xenopathogen** — life, or disease, from elsewhere, which ours has no defense against.
- **patient zero** — the first person infected, where every trail begins.
- **accelerated mutation** — a body rewriting itself in hours. (Not *genetic drift*, which in biology means random change in a population over generations.)
- **reanimation** — dead tissue moving again.
- **spontaneous human combustion** — a body said to burn from within, leaving the room nearly untouched.
- **cryptid** — an animal of report and folklore that science hasn't confirmed: Bigfoot, Mothman.

## Mind and perception
- **infohazard** — information that harms the person who learns it; a real term in risk research, a monster in fiction.
- **memetic** — spreading from mind to mind like a contagion; a **memetic hazard** is an idea that infects.
- **ESP / psi** — extrasensory perception; *psi* covers all claimed psychic ability.
- **telepathy · precognition · psychokinesis** — reading minds; knowing the future; moving things with the mind.
- **remote viewing** — perceiving distant places with the mind; the US government funded research until 1995 (the Stargate Project).
- **astral projection** — the mind leaving the body to travel.
- **psychic backlash** — the shock that rebounds on a psychic when contact goes wrong.
- **tulpa / thoughtform** — a being made real by focused belief.
- **egregore** — occult term: an entity that grows from a group's shared will or emotion.
- **hive mind** — one consciousness spread across many bodies.
- **noosphere** — the realm of human thought taken as a layer around the Earth (Vernadsky, Teilhard de Chardin); in fiction, a place you can enter.
- **morphic resonance** — Rupert Sheldrake's fringe theory that forms and habits pass between members of a species without contact.
- **subliminal** — below conscious notice; a message hidden in an image or broadcast.
- **conditioning** — behavior shaped by repetition, or by programming.
- **pareidolia** — seeing faces and figures in random shapes.
- **apophenia** — seeing patterns and links that aren't there; the investigator's occupational hazard.
- **sleep paralysis** — waking unable to move, often sensing a presence; the *Old Hag* of folklore.
- **hypnagogia** — the visions and sounds at the edge of sleep.
- **déjà vu / jamais vu** — the false feeling of having lived this before; the familiar suddenly feeling never-seen.
- **synesthesia** — a real crossing of the senses, like tasting colors or seeing sounds.

## Folklore and visitors
- **poltergeist** — a "noisy spirit": thrown objects, knocks, a disturbed house.
- **apparition** — a ghostly figure seen briefly.
- **revenant** — someone who has come back from the dead.
- **shadow people** — dark human-shaped figures glimpsed at the edge of vision.
- **doppelgänger** — a living person's double, often an omen.
- **changeling** — a child swapped by the fair folk for one of their own.
- **the fair folk** — the fae, named carefully: dangerous and bound by strange rules.
- **trickster** — a figure who disrupts, misleads and laughs; many strange encounters have the feel of one.
- **apport** — an object that appears from nowhere, from séance lore.
- **EVP** — electronic voice phenomena: voices said to appear in recordings.
- **Fortean fall** — rain of fish, frogs or stones.
- **the Hum** — a low drone some people hear in certain towns and others can't.

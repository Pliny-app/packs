# Naming

## Mythology
- Mix English words with names from any [mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mythologies): Veles (Slavic) + star → *Velestar*; Ilmarinen (Finnish) → *Ilmar Station*.
- Reach past Greek and Norse: Mesopotamian, Finnish, Slavic, Polynesian, Celtic, Aztec and Egyptian pantheons are less worn.
- Take care with living religions; a sacred name used as a brand or a villain can offend.

## Mix languages
Translate parts of the word into *two* languages and mix them.
1. World = *monde* in French
2. World = *düynö* in Kyrgyz
3. World = *Moynö* in Shamelese, the language of this world.

## Owner
- In *Star Wars*, the Czerka corporation owns the world CZ-198.
- *Atreisdea*, after House Atreides.
- After the scientist and program that discovered it: Samantha A. Lawson, United Nations Neptune → *SAL-UNN*.
- After a sponsor, a dynasty or a founder: *Hallworth's Reach, Vance Colony*.

## Reversal and anagram
- Reverse, then smooth for sound: Mercury → *Yrucrem* → *Yurcrem*.
- Rearrange the letters: Mars → *Rams, Sram*; Saturn → *Turnas, Natrus*.

## Add modifiers to known names
- New: *New Mars*.
- Roman numeral: *Mars IV*.
- Rank or size: *Mars Prime, Mars Minor, Mars Secundus*.
- Purpose: *Port Mars, Fort Mars, Mars Station, Mars Deep*.

## Translated position
- *Norra* — "northern" in Swedish.
- *Proxima* — "nearest" in Latin; *Ultima* — "farthest"; *Medius* — "middle"; *Tertius* — "third".
- Any descriptor works the same way: *Kaltmar* from German *kalt*, "cold".

## Catalogue names
- Star catalogue + number: *Gliese 581*, *HD 189733*.
- Planets get a lowercase letter, starting with *b*: *Kepler-22b*, *TRAPPIST-1e*.
- Greek letter + constellation for bright stars: *Alpha Centauri, Tau Ceti*.
- Number + constellation: *61 Cygni*.
- Colonists nickname what catalogues number: *Kepler-22b* becomes *Keppie*.

## Place-name endings
- *-by, -thorpe* — Norse farmstead, village.
- *-wick, -ton, -ham* — Old English settlement, farm, home.
- *-chester, -caster* — from a Roman fort.
- *-burg, -heim, -holm* — Germanic fort, home, islet.
- *-grad, -gorod* — Slavic city.
- *-abad, -stan* — Persian city, land.
- *-pur* — Sanskrit city.
- *-polis* — Greek city.
- Swap the culture of a familiar name: *Marsgrad, Marsabad, Marsholm*.

## Compounds and kennings
- Two plain nouns, describing the place: *Ashfall, Stormhold, Saltreach, Rimewater*.
- Kennings — the Old English trick of naming by description: the sea as *whale-road*, a ship as *wave-horse*.
- Blend two words: *Ferrovia* (iron + way), *Glasmoor*.

## Sound
- Soft sounds (*l, m, n*, front vowels *i, e*) feel gentle and light; hard sounds (*k, g, z, r*, back vowels *o, u*) feel harsh. Psychologists call this the *bouba–kiki effect*.
- Long names with open vowels feel ancient or grand: *Amaranthea*. Short, clipped names feel practical or military: *Kett, Dask*.
- Decide which feel each culture should have, and keep it.

## Set language rules
### Physiology
- Constrain sounds by physiology: a species with no lips can't produce *B, P, M* or *W*. One with a slit vocal apparatus might build every name in three beats.

### Social conventions
- Personal names start with a fricative (*f, s, sh, v, th*); clan names have two syllables, personal names three.
- Borrow real structures:
  - patronymics — *-son, -dottir, -ovich, ap, ibn*
  - occupation names — *Smith, Müller, Kowalski*
  - place names — *von, de, van*
  - epithets — *the Bold, the Unready*

### Sound change
- Derive names from an older form by consistent rules, as real languages do: if every old *p* became *f*, then *Pallan* is now *Fallan*, and every name follows the same shift.
- Older names keep older sounds; a city founded in the old tongue still says *Pallan*.

### Syllable patterns
- Choose an inventory of consonants and vowels, and a syllable shape (*CV, CVC, CVV*), then build only from those. A small, strict set sounds like a real language; a random one sounds like typing.

## Corporations
- Playful spelling: doubled letters and reduplication, as in *Google* (from *googol*), *Yahoo*, *Zoom*, *Hulu*, *TikTok* → *Vooma*, *Zingzang*.
- Dropped vowels and swapped letters, the Web 2.0 habit: *Flickr*, *Tumblr*, *Lyft*, *Fiverr*, *Digg* → *Spacr*, *Orbyt*.
- Suffixes that date a name: *-ly* (*Bitly*, *Calendly*), *-ify* (*Spotify*, *Shopify*), *-io*, *-hub*, *-AI* → *Dockly*, *Terraformify*, *Orbit.ai*.
- Portmanteaus of what the company does: *Microsoft* (microcomputer + software), *Instagram* (instant camera + telegram), *Pinterest*, *Airbnb* → *Ferrovance*, *Cryolux*.
- Founders' names, alone or paired: *Ford*, *Siemens*, *Hewlett-Packard*, *Adidas* (Adi Dassler) → *Okafor-Lindqvist*.
- Acronyms that outlive their meaning: *IBM*, *3M* (Minnesota Mining and Manufacturing), *KFC*, *IKEA* (founder, farm and village initials) → a company still called *NMT* long after anyone remembers *Neptune Mining & Transport*.
- Invented, meaningless names built to sound right: *Kodak*, *Xerox*, *Häagen-Dazs* (American-made, Danish-sounding) → *Vexora*, *Quillon*.
- Latin and Greek coinages for a premium feel: *Verizon* (*veritas* + horizon), *Accenture* → *Solvantis*, *Aeternum*.
- Myth and fiction: *Nike*, *Oracle*; defense and data firms named after Tolkien artifacts, such as *Palantir* and *Anduril* → *Gungnir Systems*.
- One short real word, the 2020s rebrand: *Meta*, *Block*, *Alphabet*, *X* → *Field*, *Thread*, *Hold*.
- Domain workarounds when the name is taken: *Booking.com*, *GetYourGuide*, *-HQ*, *-app*, *use-* → *TryOrbit*, *KettleHQ*.
- Classic sci-fi corporate patterns:
  - name + generic suffix — *Industries, Dynamics, Systems, Holdings, Consolidated, Interstellar*
  - hyphenated merger names that hint at a history — *Weyland-Yutani* in *Alien*
  - grandiose prefixes that signal satire — *Omni-*, *Pan-*, *Globo-*, *Mega-*, as in *Omni Consumer Products* in *RoboCop*

## Ships, stations and things
- Virtues and qualities: *Endurance, Resolve, Patience*.
- Heroes, places, battles: *Lawson, Port Hale, Third Cassius*.
- Registry codes: *UNN-0417*, hull classes, model numbers: *Type-7, Mark IV*.
- Whimsical full-sentence names, for a culture with a sense of humor: *Unexpectedly Pleasant Weather*.

## Checks
- Say it aloud; if readers can't, they'll skip it.
- Give main characters different first letters and lengths; readers recognize names by shape.
- Search it: in another language it may be a rude word, or already a brand.
- Use apostrophes and diacritics only if they mean something, such as a glottal stop or a stress mark. *X'th'qua* reads as a cliché.

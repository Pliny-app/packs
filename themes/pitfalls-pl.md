# English Pitfalls for Polish Speakers

Each entry gives the slip, the Polish behind it, and what a native speaker would write instead.

## Articles
Polish has no articles, so *a*, *an* and *the* go missing or land in the wrong place. This is the most visible Polish trace in written English.
- **missing a / an** — "It's beautiful place", "It's crazy city" → *It's a beautiful place, It's a crazy city*. A singular countable noun almost always needs an article.
- **missing the** — "I sent email to client" → *I sent an email to the client*.
- **the instead of a** — "He is the doctor" → *He is a doctor* (one of many). *The doctor* means a particular one, already known.
- **a instead of the** — "A film I saw yesterday was awesome" → *The film I saw yesterday…* It's a particular film, identified by the rest of the sentence.

## Tenses
- **past instead of present perfect** — Polish has one past tense where English has two. If no finished time is given, or the experience matters now, English uses the present perfect:
  - "Were you ever in Japan?" → *Have you ever been to Japan?*
  - "I was never in Paris" → *I've never been to Paris.*
  - "I saw that film" → *I've seen that film* (but *I saw it last week*, with a time).
  - "A restaurant in Warsaw I wasn't in" → *a restaurant in Warsaw I've never been to*.
- **simple instead of continuous** — "I talk to you!" → *I'm talking to you!* (happening now).
- **continuous instead of simple** — "If somebody is traveling a lot" → *If somebody travels a lot* (a habit).
- **wrong form after *to*** — the verb after *to* stays in its base form: "I want to started" → *I want to start*. Watch "I would like to found…": *found* means establish; *find* is what you meant.

## Word order
Polish word order is free; English is fixed.
- **adverb before the verb** — "I very like strawberries" → *I really like strawberries*, or *I like strawberries very much*.
- **questions inside sentences** — "Do you know where is the station?" → *Do you know where the station is?* Only direct questions invert.
- **there is · there are** — "In London are many parks" → *There are many parks in London*. "At the side of the road is a shop" → *There's a shop at the side of the road*.
- **preposition at the end** — "At what are you looking?" → *What are you looking at?* Ending with a preposition is normal English.

## Prepositions
- **na → on** — usually right, often not:
  - "on the meeting · on the conference" → *at the meeting · at the conference*
  - "on university" → *at university*
  - "on Christmas" → *at Christmas* (British; *on Christmas Day* for the day itself)
  - "on the photo" → *in the photo*
  - "on the fresh air" → *in the fresh air, outside*
  - "on the beginning" → *at the beginning* (or *in the beginning*, meaning at first)
- **w → in** — ability takes *at*: "good in playing guitar" → *good at playing guitar*.
- **call to** — from *zadzwonić do*. "I'll call to you tomorrow" → *I'll call you tomorrow*. *Call* takes no preposition.
- **congratulations for** — Write *congratulations on your promotion*.
- **think by images** — from *myśleć obrazami*. Write *think in images, think visually*.

## Negatives
Polish requires several negatives in one sentence; English uses one.
- "Nobody doesn't understand nothing" → *Nobody understands anything.*
- "I don't regret nothing" → *I don't regret anything*, or *I regret nothing*.

## False friends
- **eventually** — from *ewentualnie*, "possibly, alternatively". In English it means "in the end". Write *possibly, alternatively, if needed*.
- **actual · actually** — from *aktualny · aktualnie*, "current · currently". "This schedule is not actual" → *This schedule is out of date*. In English they mean "real · in fact".
- **sympathetic** — from *sympatyczny*, "nice, likeable". "He is very sympathetic" → *He's very nice*. In English it means compassionate.
- **realize** — from *realizować*, "carry out". "We realized the order" → *we completed the order, we fulfilled the order*.
- **control** — *kontrola* is an inspection. "We had a control from the tax office" → *a tax inspection, an audit*.
- **data** — *data* is a date. "The data of the meeting" → *the date of the meeting*.
- **evidence** — *ewidencja* is a register or records. "Evidence of working time" → *time records*. In English, *evidence* means proof.
- **pension** — *pensja* is a salary. *Emerytura* is a pension.
- **delegation** — *delegacja* is a business trip. "I'm on delegation" → *I'm on a business trip*.
- **provision** — *prowizja* is a commission. Write *commission*.
- **prospect** — *prospekt* is a brochure. Write *brochure, leaflet*.
- **concurrence** — *konkurencja* is competition. Write *competition, competitors*.
- **president** — the *prezes zarządu* runs the company. Write *CEO* or *chairman of the management board*.
- **chancellery** — a *kancelaria prawna* is a law firm. Write *law firm, law office*.
- **ordinary** — *ordynarny* means vulgar or crude. In English, *ordinary* means normal.
- **pathetic** — *patetyczny* means grandiloquent or melodramatic. In English, *pathetic* means pitiful.
- **lecture** — *lektura* is reading, or a set book. In English, a *lecture* is a talk.
- **fabric** — *fabryka* is a factory.
- **receipt** — *recepta* is a prescription.
- **transparent** — *transparent* is a protest banner. In English, *transparent* means see-through or open.
- **trainings** — from *szkolenia, treningi*. *Training* is uncountable. Write *training sessions, courses*.

## One Polish word, two English ones
- **wygodny → comfortable or convenient** — chairs and shoes are *comfortable*; tools and arrangements are *convenient*. "This PDF isn't very comfortable" → *isn't very convenient*.
- **uczyć · uczyć się → teach · learn, study** — "She learned me English" → *She taught me English*. Revising for an exam is *studying*: "I was studying until midnight."
- **zrobić → do or make** — you *make* a mistake, a decision, a call; you *do* homework, a favor, a job. "He makes it better than me" → *He does it better than me*.
- **mało → few or little** — *few* for things you count, *little* for amounts: "Few people have such a talent", "There's little time".
- **dom → house or home** — a *house* is the building, *home* is where you live: *go home*, *at home*.
- **zegar · zegarek → clock · watch** — a *clock* hangs on the wall, a *watch* goes on the wrist.

## Translated phrases
- **have right** — from *mieć rację*. "You have right" → *You're right*.
- **enter into a topic** — from *wejść w temat*. Write *get into, go into a topic*.
- **everything in order?** — from *wszystko w porządku?* Correct but formal. Write *Is everything OK? · All good?*
- **…, yes? · …, tak?** — the Polish tag question. "First coffee, then friendship, then marriage, yes?" → *…, right?* or *…, huh?* in speech.
- **how do you call it?** — from *jak to się nazywa?* Write *what do you call it?*
- **make a photo · make a pause** — Write *take a photo, take a break*.
- **please** (as a reply to thanks, or when handing something over) — from *proszę*. Write *you're welcome · here you go*.

## Grammar slips
- **since three years** — from *od trzech lat*. Write *for three years*, with the present perfect: *I've worked here for three years*.
- **until Friday** (as a deadline) — from *do piątku*. Write *by Friday*. *Until* describes a stretch of time.
- **explain me · discuss about** — from *wyjaśnij mi · dyskutować o*. Write *explain to me · discuss*.
- **informations · advices · feedbacks · equipments** — uncountable in English: *information, advice, feedback, equipment*. "The informations were useful" → *The information was useful*.

## English words used differently
- **open space** — an open-plan office.
- **home office** — working from home. In Britain, the Home Office is a government ministry.
- **mobbing** — workplace bullying. Write *bullying*.
- **smoking** — a tuxedo. On an invitation, write *black tie*.

## Emails
- **Dear Ladies and Gentlemen · Dear Sirs** — from *Szanowni Państwo*. Write *Dear Sir or Madam* for an unknown reader, otherwise *Hello* or *Dear all*.
- **Dear Mr Director** — from *Szanowny Panie Dyrektorze*. English emails use the name, not the title: *Dear Mr Kowalski*.
- **With respect** — from *Z poważaniem*. Write *Kind regards, Yours sincerely*.
- **Greetings** — from *Pozdrawiam*. Write *Best regards, Best*.

## Numbers, dates and time
- **billion** — Polish *miliard* is the English *billion*; Polish *bilion* is the English *trillion*.
- **3,5 · 1 000** — Polish uses a decimal comma and a space for thousands. English: *3.5 · 1,000*.
- **03.04.2026** — 3 April in Poland, but an American reads March 4. Write *3 April 2026*.
- **half eight** — Polish *wpół do ósmej* is 7:30, but British *half eight* is 8:30. Write the digits: *7:30*.

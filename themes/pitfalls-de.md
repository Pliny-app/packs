# English Pitfalls for German Speakers

Each entry gives the slip, the German behind it, and what a native speaker would write instead.

## False friends
- **eventually** — from *eventuell*, "possibly". In English it means "in the end". Write *possibly, maybe, if necessary*.
- **actual · actually** — from *aktuell*, "current, up to date". In English they mean "real · in fact". Write *current · currently, at the moment*.
- **become** — from *bekommen*, "get". "I became your email" → *I received / got your email*.
- **also** (opening a sentence) — from *also*, "so, therefore". "Also, let's start" → *So, let's start*.
- **control** — from *kontrollieren*, "check". "Please control the figures" → *please check*. A *Kontrolle* by an authority is an *inspection* or *audit*.
- **chef** — *Chef* is the boss. In English, a cook. Write *boss, manager, head*.
- **consequent · consequently** — from *konsequent*, "consistent, rigorous". In English, *consequently* means "therefore". Write *consistent · consistently*.
- **sensible** — from *sensibel*, "sensitive". In English, *sensible* means "reasonable". Write *sensitive*.
- **meaning** — *Meinung* is an opinion. "In my meaning" → *in my opinion*.
- **provision** — *Provision* is a sales commission. In English, a supply or a clause. Write *commission*.
- **prospect** — *Prospekt* is a brochure. In English, a likely customer or an outlook. Write *brochure, leaflet*.
- **concurrence** — *Konkurrenz* is competition. Write *competition, competitors*.
- **engagement** — *Engagement* is commitment. In English it more often means a booking or a betrothal. Write *commitment, dedication*. (*Employee engagement* is fine.)
- **personal** — *Personal* is staff. "The personal department" → *HR, personnel*.
- **art** — *Art* is a kind or type. "This art of problem" → *this kind of problem*.
- **map** — *Mappe* is a folder. Write *folder*.
- **fabric** — *Fabrik* is a factory. In English, cloth.
- **note** — *Note* is a grade. Write *grade, mark*.
- **gymnasium** — an academic secondary school. In English, a gym. Write *secondary school, grammar school*.
- **borrow** — *leihen* covers both directions. "Can you borrow me your charger?" → *lend me*.

## Translated phrases
- **make a photo · make a pause · make sport** — from *machen*. Write *take a photo, take a break, do sport / exercise*.
- **with the bus · with the train** — from *mit dem Bus*. Write *by bus, by train*.
- **on the picture** — from *auf dem Bild*. Write *in the picture*.
- **hello together** — from *Hallo zusammen* (Swiss *Hoi zäme*). Write *hi all, hello everyone*.
- **gladly** (as a reply to thanks) — from *gerne*. Write *you're welcome, happy to help*.
- **please** (as a reply to thanks, or when handing something over) — from *bitte*. Write *you're welcome · here you go*.
- **in the attachment you find** — from *Im Anhang finden Sie*. Write *attached is, please find attached*.
- **I'm in home office** — Write *I'm working from home*.

## Grammar slips
- **since three years** — from *seit drei Jahren*. Write *for three years*, with the present perfect: *I've worked here for three years*, not "I work here since three years".
- **until Friday** (as a deadline) — from *bis Freitag*. Write *by Friday*. *Until* describes a stretch of time: "I'm away until Friday."
- **how long are you here?** — from *Wie lange bist du schon hier?* Write *How long have you been here?*
- **if I would have known** — Write *if I had known*.
- **look forward to hear** — Write *look forward to hearing*.
- **explain me · discuss about** — Write *explain to me · discuss*.
- **informations · advices · feedbacks · equipments · knowledges** — uncountable in English: *information, advice, feedback, equipment, knowledge*.

## English words that aren't English
- **Handy** — a mobile phone. Write *phone, mobile*. In English, *handy* means useful.
- **Beamer** — a projector. In English, slang for a BMW.
- **Home Office** — working from home. In Britain, the Home Office is a government ministry.
- **Mobbing** — workplace bullying. Write *bullying*.
- **Shooting** — a photo shoot. In English, *a shooting* is a gun attack.
- **Public Viewing** — an outdoor screening of a match. In English, a *viewing* is a chance to see a body before a funeral.
- **Oldtimer** — a vintage car. In English, an *old-timer* is an old person.
- **Smoking** — a tuxedo. On an invitation, write *black tie*.

## Emails
- **Dear Ladies and Gentlemen** — from *Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren*. Write *Dear Sir or Madam* for an unknown reader, otherwise *Hello* or *Dear all*.
- **Friendly greetings · Best greetings · Lovely greetings** — from *Freundliche / Beste / Liebe Grüsse*. Write *Kind regards · Best regards · Best wishes*.

## Numbers, dates and time
- **billion** — German *Milliarde* is the English *billion*; German *Billion* is the English *trillion*.
- **3,5 · 1.000** — German uses a decimal comma and a thousands point. English: *3.5 · 1,000*. (Swiss amounts are often written *1'000.50*.)
- **03.04.2026** — 3 April in Europe, but an American reads March 4. Write *3 April 2026*.
- **half eight** — German *halb acht* is 7:30, but British *half eight* is 8:30. Write the digits: *7:30*.
- **KW 42** — calendar weeks are rarely used in English. Write *the week of 12 October*.

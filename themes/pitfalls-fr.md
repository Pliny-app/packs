# English Pitfalls for French Speakers

## False friends
- **eventually** — from *éventuellement*, "possibly". In English it means "in the end". Write *possibly, if needed*.
- **actual · actually** — from *actuel · actuellement*, "current · currently". In English they mean "real · in fact".
- **assist** — from *assister à*, "attend". "I assisted to the meeting" → *I attended the meeting*.
- **attend** — from *attendre*, "wait". "I attend your answer" → *I look forward to your reply*.
- **control** — from *contrôler*, "check". "Please control the figures" → *please check*.
- **demand** — from *demander*, "ask". In English, *demand* is forceful. "He demanded me to send it" → *he asked me to send it*.
- **realize** — from *réaliser*, "carry out, make". "We realized the project" → *we completed the project*. In English, *realize* means understand.
- **precise** (as a verb) — from *préciser*, "specify, clarify". "I precise that…" → *I should point out that…*
- **precisions** — *précisions* are details. Write *details, clarification*.
- **delay** — *délai* is a time limit. "Within the delay" → *by the deadline*. In English, a *delay* is a hold-up.
- **formation** — *formation* is training. Write *training, course*.
- **planning · timing** — *un planning, le timing* are schedules. Write *schedule, timeline*.
- **agenda** — *un agenda* is a diary or calendar. In English, the list of topics for a meeting.
- **foresee** — from *prévoir*, "plan, provide for". "The contract foresees a penalty" → *provides for*. In English, *foresee* means predict.
- **dispose of** — from *disposer de*, "have". "We dispose of a budget" → *we have*. In English it means get rid of.
- **punctual** — *ponctuel* means one-off or occasional. "A punctual problem" → *a one-off problem*. In English, *on time*.
- **important** — often means *large*: "an important sum" → *a large sum*.
- **actor** — *acteur* is a stakeholder or player. "All actors in the market" → *all players*.
- **collaborator** — *collaborateur* is a colleague or employee. In English it can suggest wartime betrayal. Write *colleague, team member*.
- **direction** — *la direction* is management. Write *management, leadership*.
- **responsible** (as a noun) — *le responsable* is the person in charge. Write *head of, manager*.
- **society** — *société* is a company. Write *company, firm*.
- **benefice** — *bénéfice* is profit. Write *profit*.
- **exploitation** — the running of a site or system. In English, *exploitation* is unfair use. Write *operation, operations*.
- **valorize** — from *valoriser*, "enhance, make the most of". Write *showcase, make the most of*.
- **sensible** — from *sensible*, "sensitive". In English it means reasonable.
- **sensibilize** — from *sensibiliser*. Write *raise awareness*.
- **engagement** — commitment. Write *commitment*.
- **sympathetic** — from *sympathique*, "nice, likeable". In English it means compassionate.
- **deceive** — from *décevoir*, "disappoint". In English, *deceive* means mislead.
- **pretend** — from *prétendre*, "claim". In English, *make believe*.
- **resume** — from *résumer*, "summarize". In English, *resume* means continue.
- **location** — a rental. Write *rental, hire*.
- **library** — *librairie* is a bookshop. A *bibliothèque* is a library.
- **stage** — an internship. Write *internship*.

## Translated phrases
- **me, I think** — from *moi, je pense*. French doubles the pronoun for emphasis; written English doesn't. Write *I think*, or *personally, I think* / *in my view* to stress it.
- **take altitude** — from *prendre de la hauteur*. Write *step back, zoom out, look at the big picture*.
- **make a point** — from *faire le point*, "review where things stand". In English it means argue something. Write *take stock, a status check*.
- **in the frame of** — from *dans le cadre de*. Write *as part of, under, within*.
- **at the level of** — from *au niveau de*. Write *regarding, in, for*.
- **animate a meeting** — from *animer*. Write *run, chair, facilitate*.
- **pilot a project** — from *piloter*. Write *manage, lead*. In English, a *pilot* is a trial.
- **mount a project** — from *monter un projet*. Write *set up*.
- **put in place** — from *mettre en place*. Correct but overused; *set up, introduce* are lighter.
- **it's not evident** — from *ce n'est pas évident*. Write *it's not easy, it's not obvious*.
- **normally** — from *normalement*, "it should". "Normally it's ready tomorrow" → *it should be ready tomorrow*.
- **concretely** — from *concrètement*. Write *in practice, specifically*.
- **I propose you to** — from *je vous propose de*. Write *I suggest that you, let's*.
- **make a photo · make a pause** — from *faire*. Write *take a photo, take a break*.
- **do a brainstorming** — Write *brainstorm*, or *have a brainstorm*.

## Grammar slips
- **I'm agree** — from *je suis d'accord*. Write *I agree*.
- **since three years** — from *depuis trois ans*. Write *for three years*, with the present perfect: *I've worked here for three years*.
- **explain me · say me · discuss about** — Write *explain to me · tell me · discuss*.
- **informations · advices · feedbacks · equipments** — uncountable in English: *information, advice, feedback, equipment*.

## English words that aren't English
- **open space** — an open-plan office.
- **parking** — a car park or parking lot.
- **shooting** — a photo shoot. In English, *a shooting* is a gun attack.
- **smoking** — a tuxedo. On an invitation, write *black tie*.
- **standing** — prestige, as in "high standing". Write *upmarket, luxury*.

## Emails
- **Madam, Sir** — from *Madame, Monsieur*. Write *Dear Sir or Madam*, or *Hello*.
- **thanks for your comprehension** — Write *thanks for your understanding*.
- **I remain at your disposal** — from *je reste à votre disposition*. Correct but stiff. Write *let me know if you have any questions*.
- **waiting for your return** — from *dans l'attente de votre retour*. Write *I look forward to hearing from you*.
- **please find in attachment** — from *en pièce jointe*. Write *please find attached, attached is*.
- **very cordially** — from *bien cordialement*. Write *Best regards, Kind regards*.
- **please accept the expression of my distinguished sentiments** — the long French formula has no English equivalent. Write *Yours sincerely* or *Kind regards*.
- **good day** — from *bonne journée*; sounds old-fashioned. Write *have a good day*.

## Numbers, dates and time
- **billion** — French *milliard* is the English *billion*; French *billion* is the English *trillion*.
- **3,5 · 1 000** — French uses a decimal comma and a space for thousands. English: *3.5 · 1,000*.
- **03/04/2026** — 3 April in France, but an American reads March 4. Write *3 April 2026*.
- **14h** — Write *14:00*, or *2 pm*.

Further reading: the European Court of Auditors' guide *Misused English Words and Expressions in EU Publications* (Jeremy Gardner), which covers many French-influenced usages.

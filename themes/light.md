# Light

## Bright light
- **radiant** — shining clearly and evenly; also used of a face or mood.
- **luminous** — giving off light, or catching it so it seems to.
- **brilliant** — intensely bright, often with sharp, vivid color.
- **beaming** — sending out strong, direct light; common for the sun and for smiles.
- **blazing** — fiercely bright and hot, like fire or the noon sun.
- **dazzling** — so bright it briefly overwhelms the eye.
- **blinding** — too bright to look at at all.
- **effulgent** — literary: pouring out splendid light.
- **incandescent** — glowing white-hot; figuratively, burning with feeling.

## Soft light
- **glowing** — giving a steady, soft light without flame.
- **lambent** — literary: playing softly over a surface, like candlelight on a wall.
- **haloed** — ringed by a soft band of light.
- **diffuse** — spread out and even, with no hard source or shadow.
- **afterglow** — the light left in the sky once the sun has gone.
- **golden hour** — the warm, low light just after sunrise or before sunset.
- **gilded** — touched with gold, as by late sun.
- **amber** — warm orange-gold, like lamplight through honey.

## Moving light
- **flickering** — wavering and unsteady, like a flame or a failing bulb.
- **glimmering** — faint, coming and going.
- **glinting** — a quick, hard flash of reflected light, off metal or an eye.
- **glistening** — shining because wet or oily.
- **glittering** — sparkling from many small points at once.
- **shimmering** — trembling light, as over water or rising heat.
- **scintillating** — throwing off rapid, bright sparks; of talk, brilliantly lively.
- **strobing** — flashing on and off in fast, regular pulses.

## Sources and times of day
- **daybreak** — the first light of morning.
- **dusk** — the dim stretch after sunset, before full dark.
- **sun-drenched** — soaked in strong sunlight.
- **overcast** — under full cloud; light flat and shadowless.
- **moonlit** — lit by the moon, pale and cool.
- **candlelit** — lit by candles, warm and moving.
- **fluorescent** — the flat, cool light of tube lamps; of color, unnaturally bright.
- **neon** — vivid, electric color, as from a sign.
- **phosphorescent** — glowing on in the dark, without heat, after the light source is gone; often used loosely for any eerie glow.
- **auroral** — like the northern lights, in shifting curtains of green and violet; poetic for dawn.

## Shadow and darkness
- **dim** — low in light; detail hard to make out.
- **faint** — barely there; only just visible.
- **shaded** — kept out of direct light.
- **dappled** — scattered with spots of light and shade, as under leaves.
- **penumbra** — the lighter outer edge of a shadow, half lit.
- **deep-shadowed** — heavy with shadow.
- **inky** — dense, total black.
- **murky** — dark and cloudy, hard to see into, like dirty water.
- **silhouetted** — seen only as a dark shape against something bright.
- **eclipsed** — cut off from light by something passing in front; figuratively, outshone.
- **chiaroscuro** — strong, dramatic contrast of light and shadow, as in Baroque painting.

## Hidden and unclear
- **obscured** — partly hidden from view.
- **cloaked** — wrapped out of sight, as if in cloth: cloaked in fog.
- **hazy** — softened by a thin veil of mist, dust or heat.
- **fogged** — clouded over, like a window or a lens.
- **blurred** — with smeared edges; out of focus.
- **mirage** — an image of something that isn't there, made by light bending through hot air.

## Color and sheen
- **saturated** — color at full strength.
- **chromatic** — of or having color; mostly technical.
- **color-washed** — tinted all over by one color, as through stained glass.
- **lurid** — garishly, unpleasantly vivid; also sensational.
- **alabaster** — smooth, translucent white, often of skin or stone.
- **crystalline** — clear and sparkling, like glass or ice.
- **iridescent** — colors that shift with the angle, like a soap bubble or a beetle's wing.
- **opalescent** — milky, with soft flashes of color, like opal.
- **pearlescent** — a soft, pale sheen, like the inside of a shell.
- **prismatic** — split into the colors of the rainbow.
- **holographic** — a rainbow sheen that moves as you move, like foil.
- **kaleidoscopic** — many bright, shifting patterns; figuratively, rapidly changing.
- **mottled** — blotched with patches of different tones.

## Surfaces and edges
- **reflective** — throwing back light or images.
- **sleek** — smooth and glossy.
- **jagged** — with sharp, broken, uneven edges.
- **framed** — bordered by something, such as a window, a doorway or branches.

## Mood of a scene
- **ethereal** — so light and delicate it seems unreal.
- **spectral** — ghostly; pale and insubstantial.
- **eerie** — strange in a way that unsettles.
- **sombre** — dark and muted, with a heavy mood. (US: somber)
- **stark** — bare and sharply clear, with nothing to soften it.

## Camera and optics
- **aperture** — the opening that lets light into a lens or an eye.
- **exposure** — how much light an image takes in; over- or underexposed.
- **focus** — the point where an image is sharp; in or out of focus.
- **clarity** — how crisply detail can be seen.
- **contrast** — the gap between the lightest and darkest parts.
- **highlight** — the brightest part of an image.
- **backlit** — lit from behind: edges bright, front in shadow.
- **rim-lit** — outlined by a thin line of light from behind.
- **bokeh** — the soft blur, often in round discs, of out-of-focus light behind a subject.
- **bloom** — a soft glow spilling out from bright areas.
- **halation** — a halo around bright points, often reddish on film.
- **refraction** — light bending as it passes through water, glass or air; *refracted*.
- **panoramic** — a wide, sweeping view.

## Eyes and seeing
- **gaze** — a long, steady look.
- **envision** — to picture in the mind.
- **keen-eyed** — quick to notice; sharp-sighted.
- **bleary** — of eyes: blurred and heavy from tiredness or tears.
- **myopic** — nearsighted; figuratively, unable to think ahead.
- **farsighted** — seeing far things clearly and near ones poorly; figuratively, planning well ahead.
- **bifocal** — having two lens zones, one for near and one for far.
- **horizon** — where land or sea meets the sky; the limit of sight.

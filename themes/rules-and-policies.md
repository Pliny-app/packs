# Rules and Policies

## Kinds of document
- **code of conduct** — the values and behavior expected of everyone; short and principle-led.
- **code of ethics** — the principles behind decisions; often paired with, or folded into, the code of conduct.
- **policy** — a rule on one topic, and why it exists.
- **procedure · SOP** — how to carry a policy out, step by step; *standard operating procedure*.
- **guideline** — recommended practice; advisory rather than binding.
- **standard** — a measurable requirement, such as a security or quality standard.
- **handbook · employee manual** — the collected policies, plus practical information about working there.
- **staff regulations · house rules** — formal rules of a workplace (*Personalreglement* in Swiss German).
- **charter** — a founding statement of purpose and powers.
- **terms of reference** — the scope and remit of a committee or project.
- **memo · circular** — a one-off notice of a new or changed rule.

## Parts of a policy
- **purpose** — why the policy exists.
- **scope · applicability** — who and what it covers: employees, contractors, visitors.
- **definitions** — the terms the policy uses, defined once. Defined terms are often capitalized: "the Company", "Confidential Information".
- **roles and responsibilities** — who does what, who enforces it.
- **policy statement** — the rule itself.
- **exceptions** — when the rule doesn't apply, and who may grant an exception.
- **related policies** — cross-references.
- **policy owner** — the person or team accountable for keeping it current.
- **effective date · review date** — when it starts to apply; when it must next be checked.
- **version history · revision log** — what changed and when.
- **approval** — who signed it off.
- **acknowledgement · sign-off** — the employee's signed confirmation of having read it. *Often:* proof for later that you were told.

## Obligation words
- **must · must not** — a requirement or prohibition. Plain-language guides prefer these.
- **shall · shall not** — the traditional legal form of *must*. It's now discouraged, because *shall* also predicts the future and courts have read it both ways.
- **will** — a statement of what will happen: "The company will reimburse…"
- **should** — expected, but allows judgment or exceptions.
- **may** — permitted, not required. *May not* is ambiguous (not allowed, or might not); prefer *must not*.
- **is required to · is expected to · is encouraged to** — falling strength, from duty to suggestion.
- **is prohibited · is not permitted** — a ban. **Strictly prohibited** adds emphasis, not force.

## Set phrases
- **including but not limited to** — the list gives examples, not the whole set. *Often:* the list is there to stop arguments.
- **at the company's sole discretion** — the company decides and needn't explain. *Often:* the rule bends when it suits management.
- **the company reserves the right to** — keeps an option open, such as monitoring or changing the rules. *Often:* it will, if convenient.
- **up to and including termination** — the disciplinary formula: a breach can lead as far as dismissal.
- **where applicable · as appropriate** — the rule applies only in fitting cases. *Often:* left undefined.
- **in good faith** — honestly and with good intentions; protects people who report concerns that turn out to be wrong.
- **reasonable** — "reasonable notice", "reasonable steps"; deliberately flexible, settled case by case.
- **notwithstanding** — despite: "Notwithstanding section 4…" means section 4 doesn't override this.
- **subject to** — dependent on, or limited by, something else.
- **without prejudice to** — this doesn't affect other rights or rules.
- **from time to time** — occasionally; especially "as amended from time to time": the rules may change without notice.
- **this policy does not form part of your contract** — the company can change it without your consent.
- **employees are responsible for familiarizing themselves with** — you were expected to read it.
- **failure to comply may result in disciplinary action** — the standard warning.
- **any questions should be directed to** — the contact line, usually HR or a manager.

## Contracts
- **parties** — the people or companies bound by the contract, often named once and then called "the Employer", "the Supplier".
- **recitals · whereas clauses** — the background at the start: "Whereas the Supplier provides…"
- **term · termination** — how long the contract runs; how it can be ended.
- **auto-renewal · evergreen clause** — the contract renews itself unless cancelled in time. *Often:* the cancellation window is easy to miss.
- **consideration** — what each side gives in exchange; required for a contract in common-law countries.
- **representations · warranties** — statements of fact made to the other party; promises that something is or will be true.
- **indemnify · indemnity** — to cover the other party's losses or claims arising from something.
- **limitation of liability · liability cap** — a limit on how much one side can be made to pay.
- **liquidated damages** — a sum agreed in advance as compensation for a specific breach, such as a late delivery.
- **breach · material breach** — breaking the contract; breaking it seriously enough to justify ending it.
- **cure period** — time allowed to fix a breach before the other side can terminate.
- **force majeure** — events beyond anyone's control, such as war, disaster or epidemic, that excuse a party from performing.
- **best efforts · reasonable efforts** — how hard a party must try; *best* is stronger. *Often:* argued over in court.
- **time is of the essence** — deadlines are essential terms, and missing one is a serious breach.
- **governing law · jurisdiction** — which country's law applies; which courts hear disputes.
- **arbitration** — settling disputes privately before an arbitrator instead of in court.
- **entire agreement** — the written contract replaces everything said before. *Often:* what the salesperson promised doesn't count.
- **severability** — if one clause is invalid, the rest still stands.
- **assignment** — transferring rights under the contract to someone else, usually only with consent.
- **amendment** — a change to the contract, typically valid only in writing and signed by both sides.
- **waiver · no waiver** — giving up a right; a clause saying that not enforcing a right once doesn't give it up.
- **survival** — obligations, such as confidentiality, that continue after the contract ends.
- **intellectual property (IP) assignment** — work created on the job belongs to the employer. In the US, **work made for hire**.
- **moral rights** — an author's right to be credited and to object to distortion; in parts of Europe it can't be signed away.
- **schedule · annex · appendix · exhibit** — attachments that form part of the contract.
- **counterparts** — the contract may be signed in separate copies that together count as one.
- **hereby · herein · hereto · hereinafter · thereof** — legal pointers: by this, in this document, to this, from now on called, of that.
- **mutatis mutandis** — Latin: with the necessary changes made.
- **in witness whereof** — the formula before the signatures.

## Conduct topics
- **conflict of interest** — when personal gain or relationships could sway a work decision. Usually it must be **disclosed** rather than banned.
- **gifts and hospitality** — rules on accepting presents, meals and trips, often with a value limit and a register.
- **anti-bribery · anti-corruption** — banning payments to win business, including small **facilitation payments**.
- **insider trading** — trading on confidential information about a listed company; illegal in most countries.
- **confidentiality** — keeping company and client information private, often beyond the end of employment.
- **data protection · privacy** — handling personal data lawfully: GDPR in the EU, the Data Protection Act (FADP) in Switzerland.
- **acceptable use** — what company computers, networks and accounts may be used for.
- **monitoring** — the employer checking email, internet use or devices; tightly limited by law in Europe.
- **social media** — what employees may post about work, and how to identify personal views.
- **harassment · bullying** — unwanted conduct that intimidates or degrades; **sexual harassment** is its own category.
- **discrimination · equal opportunity** — fair treatment regardless of protected characteristics.
- **diversity, equity and inclusion (DEI)** — commitments to a representative, fair workplace.
- **dress code** — what to wear. **Business casual** has no fixed meaning.
- **drugs and alcohol** — limits on use at work or before working.
- **health and safety** — duties to prevent injury, report hazards and follow safety rules.
- **workplace relationships** — rules on dating colleagues, especially across reporting lines.
- **outside employment · side activities** — whether second jobs need approval.
- **company property** — care and return of equipment.
- **expenses · travel** — what can be claimed, with what receipts and approvals.
- **whistleblowing · speak-up** — channels for reporting wrongdoing, often anonymous.
- **retaliation** — punishing someone for raising a concern. Nearly always banned outright.
- **zero tolerance** — no exceptions and no warnings for a given breach. *Often:* a warning in practice for first offenders, depending on who.

## Working time and leave
- **working hours · core hours** — the contracted hours; the hours everyone must be present.
- **flexitime** — choosing start and end times around core hours.
- **overtime** — hours beyond the contract, paid, compensated with time off, or included in salary.
- **time recording** — logging hours worked; required by law in some countries.
- **remote work · hybrid work** — working from home, fully or part of the week.
- **annual leave · vacation · PTO** — paid days off. *PTO* (paid time off) is American and often combines vacation and sick days.
- **public holidays · bank holidays** — national or regional days off; *bank holidays* is British.
- **sick leave · sick pay** — time off for illness; who pays, and for how long.
- **doctor's note · medical certificate** — proof of illness, usually required after a set number of days.
- **parental leave · maternity · paternity leave** — time off around a birth or adoption.
- **bereavement leave · compassionate leave** — time off after a death in the family.
- **unpaid leave · sabbatical** — time off without pay; a long break, often after years of service.

## Joining and leaving
- **offer letter · employment contract** — the offer; the binding agreement.
- **probation · probationary period** — a trial period with a shorter notice period.
- **notice period** — how long before leaving or dismissal takes effect.
- **resignation** — the employee leaving by choice.
- **termination · dismissal** — the employer ending the contract. **For cause**: for misconduct. **Without cause**: for other reasons.
- **redundancy · layoff** — dismissal because the role is no longer needed; *redundancy* is British, *layoff* American.
- **garden leave** — paid notice spent away from work.
- **exit interview** — a conversation about why someone is leaving.
- **reference · work certificate** — a statement about the employee's work for future employers.
- **non-compete** — a ban on working for a competitor after leaving, for a set time and area.
- **non-solicitation** — a ban on poaching clients or colleagues after leaving.
- **NDA** — a non-disclosure agreement; keeps information confidential.

## Discipline and process
- **misconduct · gross misconduct** — a breach of the rules; a breach so serious it can mean immediate dismissal, such as theft or violence.
- **investigation** — gathering facts before any decision.
- **suspension (with pay)** — removal from work during an investigation; described as "not a disciplinary measure".
- **disciplinary hearing · meeting** — the formal meeting to discuss the allegations; the employee usually may bring a colleague or representative.
- **verbal warning · written warning · final written warning** — the usual escalation before dismissal.
- **performance improvement plan (PIP)** — formal goals and a deadline for underperformance. *Often:* the first step out.
- **grievance** — a formal complaint by an employee.
- **appeal** — the right to challenge a disciplinary decision.
- **due process · fair procedure** — the right to know the allegation, respond, and be heard by someone impartial.

## Premises and facilities
- **premises** — the buildings and grounds the rules apply to.
- **access card · badge · key fob** — what opens the doors; usually personal and not to be lent.
- **tailgating** — following someone through a secured door without badging in.
- **visitor policy** — sign-in, visitor badges and **escorts** for guests.
- **reception · front desk** — where visitors and deliveries arrive.
- **building hours · out-of-hours access** — when the building is open; the rules for using it outside those times.
- **lone working** — rules for people working alone, especially at night or in risky areas.
- **hot desking · desk booking** — no fixed desks; reserving one in advance.
- **clean desk policy** — clearing papers and devices at the end of each day.
- **meeting room booking** — reserving rooms; rules on no-shows and overruns.
- **quiet zone · focus area** — places for silent work.
- **kitchen · canteen rules** — cleaning up, labeling food, the fridge clear-out day.
- **lockers · storage** — personal space for belongings, and what may be kept there.
- **parking · bike storage** — who may park, where, and how spaces are allocated.
- **smoking area** — the only place smoking or vaping is allowed.
- **lost property** — where found items go, and how long they're kept.
- **CCTV** — camera surveillance, with signs and rules on who may view footage.
- **authorized personnel only** — the sign on restricted areas.
- **facility management** — the team responsible for the building, cleaning and repairs.
- **damage report · maintenance request** — how to report a fault or breakage.

## Safety and emergencies
- **evacuation · fire drill** — leaving the building in an emergency; the practice run.
- **assembly point** — where everyone gathers after an evacuation.
- **fire warden · first aider** — staff trained to manage an evacuation; staff trained in first aid.
- **emergency exit · keep clear** — escape routes and the signs protecting them.
- **incident · near miss** — an event that caused harm; one that almost did. Both must usually be reported.
- **risk assessment** — identifying hazards and how to control them before work starts.
- **PPE** — personal protective equipment: helmets, gloves, goggles, hearing protection.
- **lockout/tagout** — isolating a machine's power with locks and tags before maintenance, so no one can switch it on.
- **authorized operator · certified operator** — only trained people may use a given machine.
- **out of order** — the tag on broken equipment that must not be used.

## Equipment and IT
- **company-issued equipment** — laptops, phones and tools provided by the employer and still owned by it.
- **asset tag · asset register · inventory** — the label on each item; the record of what the company owns and who has it.
- **check-out · check-in** — signing equipment out and returning it, as with a pool laptop or a camera.
- **equipment loan agreement** — the employee's signed acceptance of responsibility for an item.
- **fair wear and tear** — normal deterioration through use, which the employee isn't charged for.
- **loss · theft · damage reporting** — how quickly losses must be reported, and to whom.
- **return of property** — handing everything back when leaving.
- **BYOD** — *bring your own device*: rules for using personal phones and laptops for work.
- **helpdesk · ticket** — the IT support desk; a logged request.
- **acceptable use** — what systems may be used for (see Conduct topics).
- **password policy · MFA** — rules for passwords; multi-factor authentication, a second check such as a code.
- **VPN** — a secure connection to company systems from outside.
- **encryption** — scrambling data so only authorized people can read it.
- **access rights · least privilege** — who can open what; giving each person only the access they need.
- **admin rights · software installation** — whether employees may install programs themselves.
- **licensed software** — only properly licensed programs may be used.
- **patching · updates** — installing security fixes promptly.
- **backup** — copies of data kept in case of loss.
- **data classification** — labeling information by sensitivity, usually **public · internal · confidential · restricted**.
- **phishing** — fake messages that trick people into giving away passwords or data; usually to be reported, not deleted.
- **security incident** — any breach or suspected breach, to be reported at once.
- **decommissioning · disposal · data wiping** — retiring equipment safely and erasing its data first.

## Words about the register
- **legalese** — dense legal wording.
- **plain language** — writing so readers can understand a rule on first reading: short sentences, *you*, *must*.
- **boilerplate** — standard text reused across documents.
- **fine print** — the details few people read; often where the limits are.
- **catch-all clause** — a broad clause covering whatever the rest didn't.
- **box-ticking** — following procedure for its own sake. *Compliance theater*: rules that look strict but change nothing.
- **policy creep** — rules multiplying over time, each one patching the last.
- **the letter vs. the spirit of the law** — what a rule says vs. what it was meant to achieve.
